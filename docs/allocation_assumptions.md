# Allocation assumptions and sensitivity (AN-7, AN-8)

Generated by `analytics/optimise/allocation_sensitivity.py`. **All values are stated assumptions for a synthetic teaching case, not Ministry logistics data.** The sensitivity tables show how much each one matters.

## Model (Section 6.3)

Minimise the severity-weighted unmet demand subject to u_dr >= yhat_dr - s_dr - x_dr, total cost <= B, an equity floor x_dr >= alpha * p_d * sum_d' x_d'r, and a transport capacity per division. Planning demand yhat is the forecast q90 summed over the 4-week horizon. Reported weighted unmet demand is **cost-weighted** (each unit counts its unit cost, so doubling every cost doubles it), which keeps 8,000 cheap ORS sachets from outweighing a few hundred NS1 kits; s is the latest stock snapshot per facility, summed to district.

- Costs are relative units (1 = one IV saline unit), not prices. Beds are not shippable, so they are not allocated.
- Volume: 1.0 = one 500 ml IV saline bag; an NS1 kit and an ORS sachet are assumed to be 10% and 5% of that.
- **Budget** = 50% of the cost of all unmet need at q90 demand (5,716.4 cost units here), so the budget scales with the scenario.

## What alpha and the coverage floor mean for policy (conflict C3)

- alpha is a floor on each district's share of each resource relative to its population share: with alpha = 0.2 a district gets at least 20% of its proportional share of every item, whether or not it is short. It is blunt: at high alpha it sends stock to districts that already have enough.
- The coverage floor (min-cover = 0.25) guarantees every district at least that share of its own unmet need is met. It targets need directly and costs less efficiency per unit of equity gained, so it is the recommended equity tool.

## Severity weights vs equal weights

Optimised under one weighting, evaluated under both. A plan optimised for equal weights loses against severity-weighted unmet demand, and the optimiser's advantage over the proportional baseline shrinks when severity weights are removed. With equal weights the cost-weighted objective is flat: any plan that spends the whole budget on real need leaves the same unmet cost, so the remaining advantage is only the waste the proportional baseline causes by shipping to districts that already have stock.
