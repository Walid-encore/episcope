# Demand-conversion assumptions (AN-6)

Generated by `analytics/optimise/demand.py`. **These values are the parameters of the synthetic data generator (`generator/model.py`), not clinical guidance. Real protocols differ; present them as stated assumptions.** `assumption_sensitivity()` shows how much each one moves the totals.

| Quantity | Dengue | Diarrhoeal | Malaria | ILI | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| los_days | 5.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 | Mean length of stay (days) |
| saline_units_per_case | 1.12 | 0.36 | 0.25 | 0.06 | IV saline units per case |
| ns1_tests_per_case | 0.6 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | NS1 tests per case |
| ors_sachets_per_case | 0.0 | 4.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | ORS sachets per case |
| fallback_admission_rate | 0.28 | 0.12 | 0.25 | 0.06 | Admission rate used if a district has no history |

- NS1 wastage: 0% extra kits.
- Admission rate: observed admitted/cases per district and disease, shrunk toward the national rate with k = 50 pseudo-cases.

## Formulae

- admissions = cases x admission_rate
- beds = admissions x length_of_stay / 7 (Little's law, steady state)
- IV saline = cases x saline units per case
- NS1 kits = cases x tests per case x (1 + wastage)
- ORS sachets = cases x sachets per case

## Scope and uncertainty

- Source: generator/model.py (ITEM_COEF per encounter, ADMIT_P, 5-day stay). Malaria and ILI need beds and IV saline only. Planning horizon: 4 weeks; planning demand: q90.
- `*_q90` columns apply the same formulae to the forecast's 90th percentile (forecast uncertainty only).
- Forecast quantiles are integers, so q90 can fall below the mean for low-count series; q90 demand is floored at the mean (q10 capped at it). Summing weekly q90 values over 4 weeks assumes the weeks move together, which is conservative.
- Beds are peak weekly occupancy over the horizon; consumables are summed over weeks and diseases.
- Beds are the extra load from these four diseases, not total occupancy: background admissions from other conditions are not modelled. Antimalarial courses are not converted.
- Not modelled: carry-over of patients admitted before the forecast window, admission rates rising during outbreaks, and district-specific clinical practice.
